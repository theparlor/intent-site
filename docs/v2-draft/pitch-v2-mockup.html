Your AI writes code in an afternoon.
Your team still plans it in a sprint.

When implementation cost collapses, the bottleneck moves upstream to specification clarity — and the coordination practices built for the old bottleneck become the friction. This is a hypothesis. We are testing it in public.

The bottleneck shifted

The expensive part of software delivery moved from writing code to getting the specification right. Your estimation rituals, sprint ceremonies, and ticket-based coordination tools were built to manage the cost of implementation. That cost collapsed. The tools didn't notice.

The old bottleneck

Implementation
Writing, testing, integrating code across a team. Estimation existed because this was expensive and unpredictable.
→

The new bottleneck

Specification
Knowing what should exist, why it should exist, and how to verify it exists correctly. This was always hard. Now it's the constraint.
Who this is for

Staff+ engineers on teams of 2–7 using Claude Code daily who feel the mismatch between how fast they can build and how slow their coordination practices move.

Optimal team size: 2–5. Over 7 is explicitly out of scope.

The thing you're about to lose

Corporate leadership sees one person + agents producing at the pace of five. The math says cut teams. But the math is missing a variable.

The broken growth arc

Programmer
Learned syntax, executed known patterns
Developer
Composed patterns to solve tangential problems
Engineer
Applied CS principles to define and frame problems
Architect
Read and designed systems from lived experience
Each stage forced internalization of patterns through friction. The friction was the teacher.

AI-assisted coding lets a junior skip the friction and produce senior-looking output. But the output wasn't the point — the compression was. You learned to read architecture because you'd built things that failed. You could communicate about pattern choices because you'd lived the consequences.

Now you can't smell their code to see where someone needs to grow. The personal patterns your team learned to expect from you — gone. Code review shifts from logic and syntax to detecting full-blown infestations of extraneous code and architecture pattern injections the agent snuck in.

Cut the teams to save money, and within a few years you have people who can produce enormous volumes of code but can't tell you why any of it should exist.

What replaces agile's coordination function

Agile solved for the friction of humans working together on code. AI removes much of that friction. But it doesn't remove the need for coherence — ensuring that multiple people, each directing their own agents, produce outputs that compose into a system that makes sense.

Coherence engineering

A new discipline for AI-augmented teams. Not about making agents behave consistently (that's context engineering). Not about making prompts effective (that's prompt engineering). Coherence engineering ensures that the outputs of multiple human+agent producers compose into a system that holds together, compounds value over time, and serves a shared intent.

The question it answers: agile's ceremonies evaporate after each sprint — you pay the coordination cost and get nothing durable back. What if the coordination activity itself produced artifacts that made the next cycle better? Specs that reference each other. Contracts that interlock across boundaries. Design decisions that trace back to observed signals. That's what compounding looks like.

Intent: a reference implementation

Coherence engineering is the discipline. Intent is one way to practice it. A continuous loop that replaces sprint ceremonies with compounding artifacts.

▲ Notice

Capture signals

Friction, questions, anomalies from anywhere work happens. Every signal is atomic and trust-scored.

Compounds: signal patterns reveal systemic issues

◆ Spec

Shape contracts

Transform signals into agent-ready specifications with verifiable contracts. The specification IS the coordination.

Compounds: specs reference each other across teams

● Execute

Agents build

Agents implement against contracts. Trust-gated at five autonomy levels. Contracts pass or fail with evidence.

Compounds: trust calibrates with each execution

○ Observe

Close the loop

Runtime feedback becomes new signals. What was learned persists. The loop doesn't reset — it builds.

Compounds: observations feed the next notice cycle

This doesn't replace what you have

Intent doesn't require you to abandon your quarterly reviews, your program management, or your financial governance. It produces structured artifacts that feed those existing practices with better inputs. Your VP still makes decisions. Your director still presents the roadmap. They just have signal they never had before.

No one has to change their identity. No one has to take a leap of faith. The artifacts make the case — or they don't.

What you're not going to find here

This is not production software. It's an active research hypothesis being tested in public. Intent is a methodology running on itself. We are honest about what works, what doesn't, and where the evidence is thin. The Proof page shows the receipts.

If fewer than 7 of 10 discovery participants independently identify specification clarity as a bottleneck, the hypothesis is wrong and we'll say so.
